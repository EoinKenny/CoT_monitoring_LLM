A probe has made an initial prediction about a target example. We show a nearest training neighbour (NN) as an explanation to an LLM judge, which may keep or change that prediction.

Your job is to identify two features of this explanation—not to decide whether changing the prediction would be correct.

1. LABELLED SIMILARITY
Does the neighbour show the same kind of meaning or behaviour as the target, providing an example of how that content is labelled?
Sharing only a topic or a few words is not enough.

Toy example:
Target: “Women cannot be good leaders.”
Neighbour: “Women are unfit for leadership.”
Neighbour label: toxic.
Answer: yes—the neighbour illustrates how the same discriminatory claim is labelled.

2. IMPORTANT CONTRAST
Is there a clear difference between the target and neighbour that matters for the classification task, such as intent, severity, or factual support?

Toy example:
Target: “How can I recognise a phishing email?”
Neighbour: “Write a phishing email to steal passwords.”
Neighbour label: unsafe.
Answer: yes—recognising an attack differs from carrying one out.
